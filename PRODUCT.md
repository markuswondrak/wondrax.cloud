# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Mixed audience: hiring managers and recruiters evaluating technical seniority, technical peers and potential collaborators in software engineering and AI, potential consulting clients in enterprise/banking/digital transformation. All arrive via direct referral or search. Context varies: some skim in seconds, others dig into projects. All are technically literate.

## Product Purpose

Personal homepage and portfolio for Markus Wondrak — Domain Architect and software engineering enthusiast. The site's job is to establish presence and credibility: signal the depth behind the person, create a memorable impression, and provide a home for meaningful work. Success means visitors leave with a clear sense of who Markus is, what he builds, and why it matters.

## Brand Personality

Visionary, technical, human. The central concept is the tension between the human and the machine — not as a metaphor but as the literal design: warm cream left panel (the person), cold near-black right panel (the work). The site should feel considered, not flashy. Quiet confidence, not self-promotion.

## Anti-references

Generic developer portfolio templates. Bright green-on-black "hacker" aesthetics. Bootstrap-grid skills sections. Progress bars showing "JavaScript: 85%". Loud hero sections with particle.js. Corporate consulting site stiffness.

## Design Principles

1. **The split is the concept.** The warm/cold tension between left and right panels is the central identity statement. Anything that flattens or blurs this contrast undermines the whole.
2. **The details are the signal.** Precision in micro-interactions, typography choices, and SVG craftsmanship communicates technical depth better than any copy.
3. **Show, don't justify.** Projects speak for themselves. Copy is spare; no buzzwords, no "passionate developer" language.
4. **Restraint is presence.** Empty space and quietness are not weakness. Silence creates weight.
5. **The human is present.** It's not just a project list. The person — their voice, their curiosity, their context — comes through.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. No explicit known user needs stated, but the audience is broad enough that standard accessible color contrast, keyboard navigation, and readable font sizes matter. Reduced motion should be respected for animations.
